Administrative / Records Management

Core Functions: receiving, recording, classifying, routing, filing, retrieving, protecting, retaining and properly disposing of records.

Accuracy Principle: Always verify names, dates, figures, signatures, attachments and references before releasing documents.

Confidentiality & Privacy: Access only when authorized; follow RA 10173 (Data Privacy Act). Classify records as: Public / Internal / Confidential / Restricted. Secure confidential records in locked cabinets or password-protected folders.

Good Practices:

  • Use tracking log / routing slip for all incoming/outgoing documents
  • Clear and consistent file naming conventions
  • Regular backups (digital and physical)
  • Controlled access to records room
  • Follow retention schedules and proper disposal authority

Common Forms Used:

  • Routing Slip — tracks document movement between offices
  • Transmittal — forwards documents to another office with "THRU" line for chain of command
  • Action Sheet — assigns specific action to designated officer
  • Records Disposition Schedule — guides retention and disposal
  • DTR (CS Form 48) — Daily Time Record, official attendance
  • Logbook — records all incoming/outgoing communications

DepEd School Forms:

  • SF 1 — School Register
  • SF 4 — Monthly Report of Attendance
  • SF 7 — School Personnel Assignment List and Basic Profile

Document Processing Rule (RA 6713): Documents must require no more than 3 signatories as far as practicable to reduce red tape.

Civil Service & Employment

Basic Principle: Merit and Fitness is the fundamental principle in career service appointments (Constitution, Art. IX-B, Sec. 3).

Employment Statuses:

  • Permanent — meets all qualification standards (education, training, experience, eligibility); has security of tenure
  • Temporary — meets all except eligibility; valid for not more than one (1) year
  • Provisional — issued when no eligible is available
  • Coterminous — ends with the term of the appointing official
  • Fixed Term — specific period stated in the appointment
  • Substitute — fills position of regular employee on extended leave
  • Casual — short duration, seasonal, piece-work basis
  • Contractual / COS / JO — no employer-employee relationship; not entitled to leave credits, 13th month pay, PERA, security of tenure
  • Probationary — 6-month trial period

Key Distinctions:

  • Appointment — official act of designating someone to a position
  • Position — specific job with defined duties and salary grade
  • Qualification Standard — minimum requirements for the position
  • Eligibility — civil service qualification one must possess (e.g., CS Professional)
  • Security of Tenure — can only be removed for cause and after due process
  • Plantilla of Personnel — official list of authorized, funded regular positions

Key Laws:

  • RA 7305 / RA 11466 — Salary Standardization Laws
  • RA 10524 — Magna Carta of PWDs; mandates 1% employment quota in government
  • RA 11313 — Safe Spaces Act; workplace safety & respect
  • EO 64, s.2024 — updates salary schedule in 4 tranches
Omnibus Rules on Leave

Found in CSC MC No. 41, s. 1998 as amended by CSC MC No. 6, s. 2020.

Standard Leave Credits: 1.25 days VL + 1.25 days SL per month of actual service = 15 days VL + 15 days SL per year.

No Leave Credits Earned When: On Leave Without Pay (LWOP) for an entire month.

Major Leave Types:

  • Vacation Leave (VL) — must be applied at least 5 days in advance; subject to "necessities of the service"
  • Sick Leave (SL) — for illness; >5 consecutive days requires Medical Certificate
  • Maternity Leave (RA 11210) — 105 days full pay (live birth); 60 days full pay (miscarriage/termination). Solo parents get +15 days. Can allocate up to 7 days to the father.
  • Paternity Leave (RA 8187) — 7 days full pay per delivery; max 4 children; married male employees only
  • Solo Parent Leave (RA 8972/RA 11861) — 7 days per year; after at least 6 months service
  • VAWC Leave (RA 9262) — 10 days paid leave for victims of violence
  • Special Leave Benefit for Women (RA 9710) — up to 2 months full pay for gynecological surgery
  • Special Privilege Leave (SPL) — 3 days per year; non-cumulative, non-commutative
  • Study Leave — up to 6 months; requires at least 2 years continuous service with VS performance
  • Rehabilitation Leave — for work-related injuries; max 6 months full pay
  • Relocation/Transfer Leave — when transferring residence due to reassignment
  • Mandatory/Forced Leave — 5 days per year required for employees with 10+ days VL credits

Teachers' Special Case: Instead of VL/SL credits, teachers receive Proportional Vacation Pay (PVP) during summer and Christmas breaks. Services rendered during vacation earn Service Credits to offset absences.

Terminal Leave: Unused leave credits may be monetized/commuted upon separation or retirement.

Monetization: Option to convert excess VL/SL credits to cash in times of emergency, subject to DBM approval.

AWOL / Habitual Absenteeism: Continuous unauthorized absence of 30 working days or more = dropped from the rolls.

Grammar — Error Recognition Rules

Subject-Verb Agreement:

  • Singular subjects → singular verbs: Each, Every, Everyone, Nobody, One of, titles, measurements, money, time, diseases, subjects (Physics, Math, Measles)
  • A number of = PLURAL; The number of = SINGULAR
  • Neither…nor / Either…or → verb agrees with the nearest subject
  • Collective nouns (committee, jury): singular as a unit; plural as individuals
  • Police, cattle = always PLURAL
  • Intervening phrases (along with, together with, as well as) do NOT change the subject number
  • More than one = SINGULAR verb
  • Bread and butter (one unit) = SINGULAR; Hard work and perseverance (two qualities) = PLURAL

Prepositions & Idioms:

  • similar TO, senior TO, junior TO, preferable TO
  • capable OF, responsible FOR, good AT, afraid OF
  • interested IN, based ON, concerned ABOUT/WITH
  • DESPITE = NO "of" (use "In spite of" with "of")
  • Look forward TO + -ing (to = preposition, not infinitive marker)
  • Accustomed TO + -ing

Common Errors:

  • Redundancy: Return back → Return; Supposing if → If/Supposing; Advance planning → Planning
  • Subjunctive: If I WERE (not was) for wishes/hypotheticals
  • Double comparison: NEVER "most smartest" → "smartest" only
  • Unless: already negative → DO NOT add "do not"
  • Parallelism: same form in lists (reading, writing, swimming — NOT to swim)
  • Pronoun case: Between you and me (not I); object of preposition needs object pronoun
  • Split infinitive: "to carefully read" — better: "to read carefully"
  • Adverb vs Adjective: walked slowly (not slow); explained clearly (not clear)
  • Indirect speech tense shift: "will" → "would" when reporting verb is in past tense
  • Data = plural (datum = singular); but often accepted as singular mass noun now

Discrepancy rule: When words vs figures differ in amount, the amount in words prevails.

Verbal Ability

Components tested: Word meaning / synonyms / antonyms, sentence completion, correct usage, paragraph organization, reading comprehension.

Tips for Word Meaning: Look for context clues in the sentence. Break down words into roots, prefixes, suffixes.

Common Prefixes:

  • pre- = before (preview, predict)
  • post- = after (postpone, postgraduate)
  • re- = again (review, return)
  • un- / dis- / in- / im- = not (unfair, disagree, inactive, improper)
  • mis- = wrong (misunderstand, misplace)
  • inter- = between (interact, international)
  • trans- = across (transfer, transport)

Sentence Completion Tips: Read the entire sentence first; identify the logical relationship (cause-effect, contrast, similarity); choose the word that makes the sentence complete and logical.

Paragraph Organization: Look for the topic sentence first (usually introduces the main idea). Then find supporting sentences that follow logically. Transition words (however, therefore, moreover, in addition, finally) help identify the sequence.

Reading Comprehension Tips:

  • Read the questions first, then read the passage
  • Identify the main idea vs. supporting details
  • Answers are usually in the text — avoid over-speculating
  • Choose the most precise and formal expression suitable for government writing
Ethics / RA 6713 — Code of Conduct

Core Principle: "Public office is a public trust."

8 Norms of Conduct:

  1. Commitment to Public Interest — Always prioritize public interest over personal; use government resources efficiently, honestly, economically
  2. Professionalism — Highest degree of excellence, intelligence, skill, dedication
  3. Justness and Sincerity — Fair to all, no discrimination, no undue favors to relatives
  4. Political Neutrality — Service independent of party affiliation or politics
  5. Responsiveness to the Public — Prompt, courteous, adequate service; reduce red tape
  6. Nationalism and Patriotism — Loyalty to Republic; pride in local resources
  7. Commitment to Democracy — Uphold Constitution; civilian supremacy; public accountability
  8. Simple Living — Modest lives; no extravagant/ostentatious displays of wealth

Prohibited Acts and Transactions:

  • Financial/Material Interest: Cannot have interest in any transaction requiring approval of their office
  • Outside Employment/Private Practice: Cannot own/control/manage/accept employment in private enterprise regulated by their office
  • Solicitation/Acceptance of Gifts: Prohibited from soliciting or accepting gifts, gratuities, favors of monetary value in connection with official duties. Exception: nominal/insignificant tokens during traditional celebrations without strings attached.
  • Misuse of Confidential Information: Cannot use or divulge confidential info for private advantage
  • Post-Employment Restriction: 1 year ban after leaving service — cannot practice profession on matters they acted upon, nor represent private entities before their former office
  • Nepotism/Conflict of Interest: Disclose relationships and inhibit from processes involving relatives

Mandatory Duties & Deadlines:

  • Respond to letters/requests: Within 15 working days from receipt. If cannot resolve, send acknowledgment explaining delay and expected completion date.
  • Max 3 signatories per document to reduce red tape
  • Frontline service: Attend promptly to anyone availing of services, even if it extends slightly beyond closing time

SALN Requirements:

  • All public officials/employees must file (except laborers and casual/temporary workers)
  • Within 30 days after assumption of office
  • On or before April 30 every year thereafter
  • Within 30 days after separation from service
  • Must declare: real properties, personal properties, liabilities, business interests and financial connections

Penalties: Administrative (fines, suspension up to 1 year, dismissal); Criminal (up to 5 years imprisonment, disqualification from public office).

Awards: CSC administers Lingkod Bayan / Dangal ng Bayan awards for exemplary service.

Government Procurement (RA 9184 & RA 12009)

RA 9184 = OLD Government Procurement Reform Act of 2003
RA 12009 = NEW Government Procurement Act of 2024 — CURRENT MAIN LAW

Principles: transparency, competitiveness, efficiency, proportionality, accountability, participatory, sustainability, professionalism.

Key Bodies:

  • BAC (Bids and Awards Committee) — 5 to 7 members; appointed by HOPE; term = 1 year renewable
  • HOPE (Head of the Procuring Entity) — appoints BAC; issues Notice of Award; CANNOT be a BAC member
  • TWG (Technical Working Group) — created by BAC; assists in eligibility screening, bid evaluation, post-qualification
  • BAC Secretariat — administrative support; headed by permanent official designated by HOPE
  • PhilGEPS — Philippine Government Electronic Procurement System; central electronic portal

Observers: At least 3 (1 from COA, 2 from private sector/NGOs). Must be invited at least 5 calendar days before procurement activity. If they fail to attend despite notice, BAC can proceed.

Jury Duty Principle: BAC/TWG/Secretariat members must give utmost priority to procurement assignments over all other duties until completed.

Quorum: Majority of all BAC members, with Chair or Vice-Chair present.

Pre-Bid Conference: Mandatory if ABC ≥ &peso;1,000,000 for Goods.

Bid Evaluation:

  • Goods & Infrastructure: determine LCB (Lowest Calculated Bid)
  • Consulting Services: determine HRB (Highest Rated Bid) — technical expertise over lowest cost
  • Bids exceeding ABC are automatically disqualified
  • Words vs figures discrepancy: amount in words prevails

Post-Qualification: After determining LCB, BAC verifies all statements. If passed, declared as LCRB (Lowest Calculated Responsive Bid).

Single Calculated and Responsive Bid (SCRB): When only one bidder submits and passes all criteria, award can be recommended.

Failure of Bidding: When no bids received; or all bids fail to comply; or winning bidder refuses award.

After 2 consecutive failed biddings: May recommend Negotiated Procurement.

Performance Security: Winning bidder must post before contract signing to guarantee faithful performance.

Blacklisting: Administrative penalty prohibiting from all government procurement activities. BAC recommends; HOPE approves.

Conflict of Interest: Chief Accountant or personnel who approve vouchers cannot be regular BAC members.

RA 12009 New Features: Stronger digitalization mandate, video recording of proceedings, open contracting data, fit-for-purpose, strategic procurement planning, market scoping, lifecycle cost.

Computer / Office Skills

Microsoft Excel Essentials:

  • All formulas begin with = (equal sign)
  • =SUM(range) — adds values
  • =AVERAGE(range) — finds average
  • =COUNT(range) — counts numeric cells
  • =COUNTA(range) — counts non-empty cells
  • =COUNTIF(range, criteria) — counts cells matching criteria
  • =SUMIF(range, criteria, sum_range) — sums cells matching criteria
  • =VLOOKUP(value, table, col_index, FALSE) — looks up value in table
  • =IF(logical_test, value_if_true, value_if_false) — conditional logic
  • =CONCATENATE() or & — joins text strings
  • =NOW() — current date and time
  • =ROUND(number, 2) — rounds to 2 decimal places
  • $A$1 — absolute cell reference (doesn't change when copied)
  • ### error — column too narrow; widen it

Useful Excel Features:

  • Data Validation — restrict what can be entered in a cell
  • Freeze Panes — keep headers visible while scrolling
  • AutoFilter / Sort — organize and filter data
  • Pivot Table — summarize large datasets quickly
  • Format Painter — copy formatting to other cells

Keyboard Shortcuts:

  • Ctrl + Z — Undo
  • Ctrl + C / V / X — Copy / Paste / Cut
  • Ctrl + Shift + Down Arrow — select entire column to last active cell

MS Word Skills: Formatting, mail merge, proper layout, tables, styles, headers/footers, page numbering.

Computer Security:

  • Strong passwords; never share credentials
  • Backups in separate location
  • Phishing awareness — verify senders, don't click suspicious links
  • Verify attachments before opening
  • Lock computer when leaving workstation

Email Etiquette: Clear and specific subject line; correct recipient; check attachments before sending; professional tone; proper greeting and closing.

File Management: Consistent naming convention; logical folder structure; version control; retention and disposal rules per policy.

DRRM — Disaster Risk Reduction & Management

Key Law: RA 10121 — Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act of 2010.

DepEd Order: DepEd Order No. 37, s. 2015 — institutionalized Comprehensive DRRM in Education Framework.

Key DepEd Office: DRRMS — Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Service.

4 Thematic Areas (RA 10121):

  1. Disaster Prevention and Mitigation — avoid hazards; reduce impact
  2. Disaster Preparedness — planning, training, early warning
  3. Disaster Response — during and immediately after; rescue, relief
  4. Rehabilitation and Recovery — restore and improve; "Build Back Better"

Disaster Risk Formula: Risk = (Hazard × Vulnerability) / Capacity

Key Terms:

  • Hazard — potential harmful event (typhoon, earthquake, fire)
  • Vulnerability — characteristics making community susceptible to damage
  • Capacity — strengths, attributes, resources to manage/reduce risk
  • Exposure — presence of people/assets in hazard zones
  • Resilience — ability to resist, absorb, adapt, and transform

Hazard Types:

  • Natural Geological — earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, landslides
  • Hydrometeorological — typhoons, floods, droughts (from atmospheric/hydrological/oceanographic processes)
  • Biological — epidemics, pest infestations
  • Human-induced / Anthropogenic — industrial accidents, structural fires, chemical spills

3 Pillars of Comprehensive Safe School Framework:

  1. Safe Learning Facilities (structural safety)
  2. School Disaster Management (policies, plans, drills)
  3. Risk Reduction / Resilience Education (curriculum integration)

Key Agencies:

  • PHIVOLCS — monitors earthquakes, volcanic activities, tsunamis
  • PAGASA — weather updates, typhoon warnings, flood advisories
  • NDRRMC — National DRRM Council
  • OCD — Office of Civil Defense

School Drills: Earthquake and fire drills must be conducted quarterly (every 3 months).

Earthquake Drill Action: Duck, Cover, and Hold.

Fire Triangle: Oxygen, Fuel, Heat. Clothes on fire: Stop, Drop, and Roll.

RADaR: Rapid Assessment of Damages Report — must be submitted within 24 to 72 hours after hazard event.

Schools as Evacuation Centers: Only as last resort. Maximum of 15 days to avoid prolonged class disruption.

Incident Command System (ICS): Standardized emergency management structure. Incident Commander has overall tactical authority.

Class Suspensions: Based on PAGASA storm warning signals per EO 66, s. 2012.

Global Framework: Sendai Framework for DRR (2015-2030) — replaced Hyogo Framework.

Priority during emergency: LIFE SAFETY FIRST; then secure critical records and assets; communicate verified information only; document everything.

EO 64, s. 2024 — Salary Update

Official Title: "Updating the Salary Schedule for Civilian Government Personnel and Authorizing the Grant of an Additional Allowance, and for Other Purposes."

Signed: August 2, 2024 by Executive Secretary Lucas P. Bersamin, by authority of President Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr.

Implementation: 4 tranches from FY 2024 to 2027:

  • Tranche 1: January 1, 2024 (retroactive)
  • Tranche 2: January 1, 2025
  • Tranche 3: January 1, 2026
  • Tranche 4: January 1, 2027

Medical Allowance: Up to &peso;7,000 per annum (yearly, NOT monthly). Primarily for HMO services. Can be given as HMO coverage OR cash (for those who pay/renew their own). DBM Budget Circular No. 2024-6 provides the rules.

COVERED: Regular / permanent / contractual with employer-employee relationship; civilian government personnel only; NGAs, LGUs, Constitutional Commissions.

NOT COVERED: Military and uniformed personnel (separate system); Job Order (JO) / Contract of Service (COS) workers (no employer-employee relationship).

LGU Implementation: Must comply with Personnel Services (PS) limitation under RA 7160 (Local Government Code). Sanggunian determines grant of Medical Allowance based on financial capability. DBM Local Budget Circular No. 160 provides guidelines.

Salary Adjustment Rules:

  • New hires start at Step 1 of the salary grade
  • Daily wage rate for casual: monthly rate ÷ 22 workdays
  • If current salary exceeds new Step 8: adjusted to Step 8 rate; if still exceeds, no salary increase entitled
  • Compulsory retirees with extended service: entitled to the increase

Funding Source: General Appropriations Act (GAA) for NGAs.

Effective upon publication in Official Gazette or newspaper of general circulation.

Situational Judgment

Decision Framework: Choose actions that are: Lawful → Ethical → Documented → Fair → Within Authority → Appropriate to Urgency.

When unsure: Check official rule → Ask proper authority → Do not guess.

Common Scenarios & Correct Responses:

  • Confidential document request: Decline unauthorized requests; explain the rule (RA 10173 / Data Privacy)
  • Supervisor asks for rule violation: Politely ask clarification; if certain it violates, explain regulation professionally; offer proper alternative; follow established channels if needed
  • Error found in submitted report: Notify proper authority; correct it; accountability and transparency required
  • Heavy workload / tight deadlines: List all tasks; prioritize by urgency and importance; use checklist; communicate early with supervisor if overwhelmed
  • Conflict of interest (relative bidding): Disclose relationship; inhibit from the evaluation process
  • Gift offered by supplier: Politely refuse; accepting gifts of value connected to official duties is prohibited by RA 6713
  • Discovered colleague stealing supplies: Report through proper channels; upholding public interest and trust is required
  • Client arrives just before closing: Attend to them promptly; frontline duty requires service even if it extends slightly past closing
  • Request cannot be resolved in 15 days: Send acknowledgment letter explaining delay, current status, and expected completion date

Common Traps to Avoid:

  • Following unauthorized instructions without verifying
  • Skipping rules or procedures for "expediency"
  • Favoritism or giving undue advantage
  • Taking shortcuts that compromise accountability
  • Blaming others instead of taking responsibility
  • Ignoring problems hoping they'll go away
DepEd Compliance & Protocols

Key Law: RA 9155 — Governance of Basic Education Act of 2001. The school level is the "heart of the formal education system."

DepEd Core Values: Maka-Diyos, Makatao, Makakalikasan, Makabansa. (Makasarili is NOT a core value.)

Key Personnel Documents:

  • 201 File — Employee's complete personnel records (appointments, clearances, diplomas, SALN, etc.)
  • Service Record — Continuous chronological history of appointments, promotions, leaves, salary steps
  • PDS (CS Form 212) — Personal Data Sheet; official profile updated regularly
  • DTR (CS Form 48) — Daily Time Record; official attendance
  • SALN — Statement of Assets, Liabilities and Net Worth
  • IPCRF — Individual Performance Commitment and Review Form (non-teaching performance evaluation)

Property Management:

  • PAR (Property Acknowledgement Receipt) — for high-value items with life expectancy > 1 year
  • ICS (Inventory Custodian Slip) — for semi-expendable items below capitalization threshold
  • RIS (Requisition and Issue Slip) — tracks distribution of supplies
  • IAR (Inspection and Acceptance Report) — verifies delivery quality and quantity
  • Delivery Receipt (DR) — supplier's document listing items delivered
  • Waste Material Report — removes worn-out/obsolete items from records
  • Notice of Loss — filed within 30 days for items lost due to calamity
  • Physical Inventory — regular check of physical presence vs book records

Financial Guidelines (MOOE):

  • MOOE — Maintenance and Other Operating Expenses
  • COA — Commission on Audit audits all transactions
  • Liquidation Report — proves cash advances were spent properly
  • No new cash advance unless previous is fully liquidated
  • Official Receipt (OR) — primary valid proof of payment recognized by COA
  • Travel cash advance must be liquidated within 10 days after return
  • Unutilized/Unliquidated balance may revert to National Treasury
  • PPMP — Project Procurement Management Plan (itemized procurement needs)
  • SIP / AIP — School Improvement Plan / Annual Implementation Plan (foundation for procurement)

Allowable MOOE Expenses: Classroom supplies, utilities (water/electricity), minor repairs, training materials, communication expenses.

NOT Allowable: Personal vacations, regular teacher salaries, major infrastructure construction.

Channel of Communication: School level issues → report first to School Head / Principal → then PSDS → then SDO.

Transmittal Memorandum: Uses "THRU" line to respect chain of command (e.g., THRU: PSDS).

Data Privacy (RA 10173): Secure 201 files in locked cabinets; controlled digital access; no sharing without consent.

Document & Memorandum Drafting

Standard DepEd Memorandum Format:

  • Letterhead: Republic of the Philippines, Department of Education, Region, Division/School name, address
  • Document Type: DIVISION MEMORANDUM / SCHOOL MEMORANDUM / OFFICE ORDER
  • Series Number: No. ___, s. 2026 ("s." = series)
  • TO: Target recipients in hierarchical order
  • FROM: Signatory official and position
  • THRU: For transmittals up the chain (e.g., THRU: PSDS)
  • SUBJECT: One concise line summarizing purpose
  • DATE: Current date
  • Body: Numbered paragraphs; purpose first, then details, then enclosure list
  • Signature Block: At the very end; approving authority signs

Key Document Types:

  • Memorandum — internal communication; policies, announcements, meetings
  • Transmittal Letter/Memo — forwards documents; has "THRU" line; lists enclosures; has "Received By" section
  • Minutes of the Meeting — objective, third person; names speakers and responses; includes agenda, attendance, discussions, decisions, adjournment
  • Request Letter — formal, courteous; states exact event, objective, timeline, fund source, attachments
  • Incident Report — facts only (Who, What, When, Where, Why); chronological; no emotional language; declares status/outcome
  • Notice of Meeting — includes date, time, venue, and numbered agenda

Writing Principles:

  • Clear, concise, objective, professional tone
  • Active voice preferred; third person for minutes/reports
  • Clean sans-serif or serif fonts (Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman)
  • Specific complete dates ("June 29, 2026" not "next week")
  • Numbered lists for multi-step instructions
  • Attachments referenced as "Enclosure" or "Annex"
  • OIC signing: sign own name over "For" or "OIC" above the title
  • Always proofread before printing/submitting
  • Archive signed original securely; keep digital backup

Routing Slip: Best practice to track document movement for multiple signatures.

"Immediate dissemination and strict compliance is desired" = share right away and follow without exception.

Spreadsheet & Financial Computation

Advanced Excel Formulas:

  • =SUMIF(range, criteria, sum_range) — sums values that meet a condition (e.g., total only "Office Supplies")
  • =IF(logical, true, false) — conditional (e.g., =IF(D10>C10, "OVER BUDGET", "WITHIN BUDGET"))
  • =IF(AND(cond1, cond2), true, false) — both conditions must be true
  • =VLOOKUP(TRIM(A2), range, col, FALSE) — fixes trailing spaces issue
  • =ROUND(formula, 2) — forces value to 2 decimal places (centavo precision)
  • Sheet reference: =January!G15 + February!G15 (references other worksheets)
  • Text joining: =B2&", "&A2 or =CONCATENATE(B2, ", ", A2)

Payroll Computations:

  • Daily wage rate = Monthly salary ÷ 22 working days
  • GSIS Personal Share = Basic salary × 9%
  • GSIS Government Share = Basic salary × 12%
  • Taxable Income = Gross income − Mandatory non-taxable deductions (GSIS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG)
  • Prorated PERA = (PERA ÷ 22) × Days actually worked
  • Salary deduction for absences = Days absent × Daily wage rate

MOOE & Liquidation:

  • Unliquidated balance = Cash advance received − Total valid liquidated expenses
  • Utilization rate (%) = (Actual disbursement ÷ Cash allocation) × 100
  • Government expects utilization between 90% and 100% per quarter

Withholding Tax Computations (VAT):

  • Net of VAT Base = Gross amount ÷ 1.12
  • 5% Final VAT Withholding = Net of VAT Base × 0.05
  • 1% Expanded Withholding Tax (EWT) = Net of VAT Base × 0.01
  • Net Payable to Supplier = Gross amount − 5% VAT − 1% EWT

Budget Computations:

  • Budget cap = Total budget × Allowed percentage (e.g., 15% for janitorial/security)
  • Electricity cost = (Current reading − Previous reading) × Rate per kWh

Financial Reporting Tools:

  • Pivot Table — best for summarizing 2,000+ row ledgers by Object Code
  • ROUND function — ensures centavo accuracy (not just visual formatting)

UACS: Unified Account Code Structure — standard government coding for classifying accounts, revenues, and expenditures uniformly.

Keep answers: clear, concise, honest, professional, rule-based, and focused on public service.

Q1: Tell us about yourself and why you want to be an Administrative Officer II.

Suggested Answer: I am a dedicated, organized, and service-oriented individual with [number] years of experience in administrative work. I hold a [degree] and have acquired Civil Service Professional eligibility. My experience includes records management, document processing, financial documentation, and coordinating with various stakeholders. I want to be an Administrative Officer II because I am passionate about public service and believe that efficient administration is the backbone of quality education delivery. I am well-versed in the laws and procedures that govern government operations, including RA 6713, procurement rules, and data privacy. I want to contribute my skills to ensure that school operations run smoothly, resources are used properly, and teachers and staff receive the administrative support they need to focus on educating our learners.

Q2: How do you handle confidential documents and sensitive information?

Suggested Answer: I strictly follow RA 10173 (Data Privacy Act) and office regulations. I only access information when authorized and necessary for official duties. I store physical documents in locked file cabinets and digital files in password-protected folders with controlled access. I never discuss sensitive details outside work or with unauthorized persons. When disposing of confidential records, I follow proper procedures such as shredding or secure deletion. I also ensure that I only share information with people who have the legal right and official need to know. Confidentiality is not just a rule for me—it is a professional obligation that protects the rights and dignity of the people we serve.

Q3: What would you do if your supervisor asks you to do something that seems against rules or regulations?

Suggested Answer: I would politely and respectfully ask for clarification and, if appropriate, cite the specific rule or policy that I believe applies. I would explain my understanding professionally and offer the proper, legal way to accomplish the task. If I am certain it violates laws or procedures, I will clearly explain the regulation and its rationale. If the instruction still conflicts, I will follow established channels to report or escalate the matter appropriately, always keeping the public interest and integrity first. I believe that open, respectful communication can resolve most issues, and that my duty is to uphold the law and the public trust above all else.

Q4: How do you organize and prioritize your work when you have a heavy workload and tight deadlines?

Suggested Answer: I list all tasks clearly, then prioritize based on urgency, importance, and deadlines using a checklist or digital tracker. I focus first on high-priority and time-sensitive items, especially those involving statutory deadlines like liquidation reports or SALN filings. I break large tasks into manageable steps. If the workload becomes too heavy, I communicate early with my supervisor about the situation, suggest possible adjustments or support needed, and propose which tasks can be rescheduled. I also keep my work area and files organized so I can work faster and avoid mistakes. Being organized allows me to meet deadlines without compromising the quality and accuracy of my work.

Q5: What is your understanding of RA 6713 or the Code of Conduct, and how will you apply it?

Suggested Answer: RA 6713 establishes that "public office is a public trust." It mandates 8 norms of conduct: Commitment to Public Interest, Professionalism, Justness and Sincerity, Political Neutrality, Responsiveness to the Public, Nationalism and Patriotism, Commitment to Democracy, and Simple Living. I will apply this by always prioritizing public interest over personal gain, performing my duties with excellence and integrity, treating everyone fairly without favoritism, refusing gifts or favors that may influence my actions, responding to the public promptly and courteously, and leading a modest lifestyle. I will also ensure that I respond to all requests within the 15-working-day deadline and file my SALN accurately and on time.

Q6: Tell us about a time you made a mistake at work and how you handled it.

Suggested Answer: In my previous work, I once made an error in a financial report due to a miscalculation. When I discovered it, I immediately informed my supervisor, took full responsibility, and corrected the error right away. I also reviewed my entire process to identify where I went wrong and implemented an additional cross-checking step to prevent recurrence. I learned that mistakes, while undesirable, are opportunities to improve systems and personal discipline. What matters is acknowledging them promptly, correcting them, and putting safeguards in place so they do not happen again. This experience made me more careful, systematic, and accountable in all my work.

Q7: How do you handle stress, pressure, and difficult situations?

Suggested Answer: I handle stress and pressure by remaining calm and focusing on what I can control. When facing multiple responsibilities, I prioritize tasks according to urgency and importance. I avoid making decisions based purely on emotions. I also believe that communication is important—if necessary, I seek guidance from people with more experience or coordinate with colleagues to find solutions. I maintain a positive attitude and see pressure as part of professional growth. Outside work, I make sure to get proper rest and maintain balance so I can stay productive and emotionally prepared.

Q8: How would you handle a difficult or irate teacher, parent, or stakeholder?

Suggested Answer: I would listen calmly and respectfully without interrupting, allowing them to express their concern fully. I would show empathy and acknowledge their feelings. Then I would address the issue objectively, explain the relevant policy or procedure clearly, and offer practical solutions or the next steps. If the matter is beyond my authority, I would properly endorse or escalate it to the appropriate official. I would never argue or respond with hostility. My goal would be to resolve the concern fairly and professionally while upholding office rules and maintaining the person's dignity.

Q9: What are your strengths and weaknesses?

Suggested Answer: My strengths include being organized, detail-oriented, and dependable. I have strong knowledge of government rules and procedures, good computer skills especially in Excel and document processing, and I work well both independently and as part of a team. I am also committed to public service and integrity. As for weaknesses, I sometimes tend to be overly concerned about making mistakes, which in the past caused me to spend extra time reviewing my work. To improve, I have learned to prepare carefully, follow standard checklists, and trust my training while still being thorough. This has helped me become more confident and efficient while maintaining accuracy.

Q10: Why should we hire you over other applicants?

Suggested Answer: You should hire me because I combine the necessary qualifications—Civil Service Professional eligibility, relevant education, and administrative experience—with a genuine commitment to public service and integrity. I have prepared thoroughly for this position by studying the relevant laws, regulations, and practical skills needed by an AO II. I am organized, detail-oriented, and can be trusted with sensitive records and government funds. I am also eager to learn and adapt to the specific needs of your school. Most importantly, I treat public office as a public trust and will perform my duties with honesty, dedication, and excellence to support the delivery of quality education to our learners.

Q11: How do you ensure accuracy in your work, especially with financial documents and records?

Suggested Answer: Accuracy is fundamental in administrative and financial work. I ensure it by following established procedures and checklists, never rushing critical tasks, and always verifying details such as names, dates, figures, signatures, and supporting documents. For financial work, I cross-check totals, reconcile amounts against official receipts, and use Excel formulas to minimize manual calculation errors. I also review my work before submission and, when possible, have a second set of eyes verify critical documents. I believe that the extra time spent ensuring accuracy prevents much larger problems later and upholds the integrity of government records.

Q12: What do you know about the duties of an Administrative Officer II in a school setting?

Suggested Answer: An Administrative Officer II in a DepEd school performs a wide range of functions supporting the School Head and the entire school community. These include: records management—receiving, recording, routing, filing, and retrieving documents; personnel action assistance—processing appointments, leave applications, service records, and 201 files; property and supply management—inventory, PAR/ICS, procurement assistance, and disposal; financial assistance—MOOE liquidation, payroll preparation support, and compliance with COA rules; drafting of memoranda, transmittals, and other communications; coordination with the SDO, PSDS, and other stakeholders; and ensuring compliance with civil service rules, RA 6713, procurement laws, and data privacy. Essentially, the AO II ensures that the school's administrative operations run efficiently, legally, and in support of the school's educational mission.

Q13: How do you handle conflict or disagreement with a coworker?

Suggested Answer: When there is conflict or disagreement, I first try to understand the other person's perspective by listening openly. I would address the issue privately, calmly, and professionally, focusing on the work concern rather than personal differences. I would look for common ground and propose solutions that serve the best interest of the office and the public we serve. If we cannot resolve it directly, I would seek guidance from our supervisor in a respectful manner. I believe that maintaining professionalism and teamwork is essential, and that most conflicts can be resolved through respectful communication and a shared commitment to our public service mission.

Q14: What motivates you to do your best in government service?

Suggested Answer: What motivates me most is knowing that my work contributes to the delivery of public service, especially in education which shapes the future of our children. When I process documents accurately, manage resources properly, or support teachers and staff efficiently, I am helping create an environment where learning can happen. I am also motivated by the opportunity to uphold the integrity of public service—showing that government employees can be honest, efficient, and dedicated. The trust placed in public servants is a privilege, and I want to honor that by doing my best every day. Continuous learning and professional growth also motivate me, as I strive to become more effective in serving the public.

Q15: Where do you see yourself 5 years from now?

Suggested Answer: Five years from now, I see myself still serving in government, having grown both professionally and personally. I hope to have mastered the duties of Administrative Officer II and possibly advanced to a higher position through merit and continued excellent performance. I envision myself as a more competent, knowledgeable, and effective public servant who can mentor newer employees and contribute to improving office systems and procedures. Regardless of position, I see myself upholding the highest standards of integrity, professionalism, and dedication to public service, and continuing to support the Department's mission of delivering quality, accessible, and liberating education to all Filipinos.

Q16: What is the most important lesson you have learned from your life experiences?

Suggested Answer: One of the most important lessons I have learned is that every challenge is an opportunity to learn and become stronger. There were times when I experienced difficulties and situations that did not go according to plan. Instead of giving up, I learned to remain patient, analyze the situation, and find ways to move forward. Personally, these experiences taught me responsibility, resilience, and understanding of others. Professionally, I apply this lesson by remaining calm when facing difficult situations, learning from my mistakes, and using my experiences to become a better employee. I believe that what matters is not how many challenges we face, but how we respond to them with integrity and perseverance.

Q17: How do you demonstrate teamwork in the workplace?

Suggested Answer: I demonstrate teamwork by communicating openly and respectfully with colleagues, sharing information and resources, and being willing to help others when I can, especially during peak periods. I respect each person's role and contribution, and I value diverse perspectives. I fulfill my own responsibilities reliably so that the team's workflow is not disrupted. When there are group tasks, I participate actively, listen to suggestions, and work toward solutions that benefit the whole team. I believe that strong teamwork in government service leads to better, more efficient service to the public.

Q18: Are you willing to render overtime work or perform other related tasks when needed?

Suggested Answer: Yes, I am willing to render overtime or perform other related tasks when the service requires it, especially during critical periods such as enrollment, liquidation deadlines, report submissions, or emergency situations. I understand that public service sometimes demands extra time and effort to meet the needs of the school and the stakeholders we serve. However, I will also ensure that I manage my time well and maintain my health and efficiency. My priority is always to deliver quality service and meet our obligations to the public.

Q19: How do you keep yourself updated with new policies and regulations?

Suggested Answer: I make it a habit to regularly read official issuances from the Civil Service Commission, DepEd, DBM, GPPB, and COA. I check the official websites and bulletins, attend seminars and training when available, and participate in office discussions of new policies. I also maintain a personal file of important laws and circulars for quick reference. I believe that continuous learning is essential in government service because rules and regulations are updated from time to time, and an effective AO II must always be knowledgeable and compliant.

Q20: Do you have any questions for us?

Suggested Answer: Yes, thank you. I would like to ask about the priorities and immediate challenges of the Administrative Office in the coming months, so I can prepare myself to contribute effectively if given the opportunity. I would also appreciate knowing what qualities or areas of performance the school values most in an Administrative Officer, so I can ensure I meet and exceed those expectations. Finally, I am interested in learning about the professional development opportunities available for administrative staff to continuously improve our skills and service.
